A man does not call a line crooked unless he has some idea of a straight line."*                                                                                — C.S Lewis, Mere Christianity

In Christianity, the problem of evil is one of the most common and difficult rebuttals to refute in debating the existence of [[God]]. This is because we believe [[God]] to be all knowing, all powerful, and all good, the existence of suffering seems contradictory to these claims; if [[God]] is all knowing, then he most know suffering exists, and if he is all powerful, then he should stop it; his failure to do so could be seen as a failure of being all good. It is worth noting this question is rooted in the concept of objective good and evil, which [[The Catholic Church|Catholics]] align with.

The Problem of Evil is typically broken into two parts: first the philosophical problem of evil, and the empirical problem of evil.

**The Philosophical (Logical) Problem of Evil**

In argument of The Problem of Evil is largely based around one central question: Why does Evil Exist? Furthermore, if Evil Exists, does it contradict the existence of an all knowing, all powerful, perfectly benevolent god?
The most common rebuttal to The Problem of Evil is argument from free-will. Without choice, is love really love? Theists (people who believe in [[God]]) argue that evil most exist so we have a choice.

**The Empirical Problem of Evil**

The Empirical Problem of Evil is one that arises typically after conceding the existence of [[God]] is logically possible. The Empirical Problem of Evil is rooted in the question: "If evil is necessary, why so much?". The existence of immense suffering in this world often leaves people feeling that there is no truly benevolent [[God]]. The most common rebuttal to this position is that all suffering goes to the building of ones character on their way to [[The Afterlife|Heaven]] (reffered to as "The Soul-Building Argument" sometimes).

>*“If God is, why is there evil? But if God is not, why is there good?”* -St. Augustine
